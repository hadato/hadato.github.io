## Goal
In 2024 I signed up for the 2000 mile long anniversary edition of the famous Czech bike-packing race 1000 Miles Adventure. To be able to enjoy the race, I wanted to change the characteristics of my suspension to better suite my needs - I mostly needed additional comfort than anything else. 

## The Suspension and Changes 
The suspension I planned to use for the race was Manitou R7 fork and Manitou Mara Inline rear shock. Both needed significant modifications to achieve optimal ride characteristics for a light rider like me on my Carbonda FM936 frame. 

## Mara Inline Rear Shock
In comparison to almost identical old Manitou Mc'Leod, the new manitou Mara has a new piston with a relatively big bleed port. This means that even when fully closed (by pressure again the compression shims), there is a flow chanel where the oil can freely flow. This improves sensitivity, however, it prevents the shock from a firm lockout.

I reached to Manitou and they send me a whole shaft/compression piston assembly. I also managed to get hold on the Mc'Leod with smaller bleed ports so I was left with a lot of shims and 3 different pistons. Both shocks and the extra compression assembly are shown below.  

![Rear shock shims]({{ "/assets/images/projects/manitou-tuning/shims.jpg" | relative_url }})

After long hours with Manitou manuals and charts, assembly, testing, disassembly, more hours with the charts, and finally a new assembly, I settled on the red piston with no bleed ports (for proper lockout) and relatively soft compression stack to boost comfort in the rough terrain.

Another big modification was a change of the air chamber to the Kink Can to boost the positive air chamber volume. I ended with 3 additional volume reducers to perfection the progressivity. 

The last modification to the shock was a swap of the lockout knob and creation of a side-entry cable location for the lockout needed for the Carbonda FM936 frame. 



## R7 Pro Fork
Manitou R7 Pro is a great fork which has been highly rated by various testers. However, the problem is that it was most likely designed as a fork with only 100 mm of travel. In the 120 mm setup, the negative chamber is way too small as discussed by [MTBR](https://www.mtbr.com/threads/2020-manitou-r7-pro-owners-thread.1142851/), resulting in a very poor small bump compliance. The solution to the problem is to reduce a plastic spacer inside the negative chamber.

![Fork shafts]({{ "/assets/images/projects/manitou-tuning/shafts.jpg" | relative_url }})

The picture above shows two almost identical air shafts - upper one from R7 and the lower one from J-unit Pro 26. The shaft from R7 Pro has already a modified spacer in the negative chamber (white, sitting in the black plastic screw). I simply filed down the plastic to a diameter of the top out rubber spacer. This modification significantly increased the negative chamber volume which resulted in much better small bump sensitivity. 

Another modification I performed was a reduction of the compression shim stack to a single shim (tuning for kids according to Manitou) which further boosted the fork compliance. 

The last modification was in the positive air chamber. The fork was too progressive so I simply removed the whole IRT (equivalent to volume spacers for other producers) and inserted one NeoPos from Formula - soft volume spacer. This turned out to be too little and the final setup had two and half of them inserted in the positive air chamber.  

With these modifications, the fork is extremely comfortable, yet has enough support in the middle of the travel due to the more linear air spring. As a bonus the for lost a bit of weight and tipped the scale just below 1600 g. Simply the best XC fork I have ever ridden.
