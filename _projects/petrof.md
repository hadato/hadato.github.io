## Goal
Restore a a nice upright piano with some history to accompany me, hopefully, for the rest of my life.

## History and Motivation
When I left Norway, I left my self-restored beloved Sauter 108 behind. After a few years without a piano, visiting public places such as Klavier Galerie, I realized that I need a piano again.

As I didn't know how my living situation will be in the near future, I decided to buy a very good Kaway VPC-1 MIDI controller backed with Pianoteq. While the feeling and sound of the combination were pretty nice, it was still not motivating enough to play it too often. The additional step of plugging in my laptop and starting Pianoteq was just in a way. While it was the best e-piano setup I have tried to this date, it was far from a real instrument. 

In November 2024 I moved to another flat in a house full of musicians and though it was a great opportunity to get a new instrument. My search began and soon I found a vintage Czechoslovakian Petrof from 1928/29 new Mladá Boleslav. Luckily, I had friends around who could help me to load it in the car so I arranged everything. One day before my moving day, I drove across Czechia to pick up my new instrument and drove back to Vienna.
The next day we moved the disassembled piano up 3 flights of stairs and my journey with the instrument began. 

## Restoration
While the soundboard and the tuning board were in a good shape, the rest of the piano was not so much. It belonged to a hobby artist composing music for movies and so it had been heavily played. I know what was coming - a full restoration of the instrument. Complete rebuild of the mechanics, including hammer reshaping, re-bushing and re-surfacing of the keys (unfortunately the original ivory is badly cracked and bent).
